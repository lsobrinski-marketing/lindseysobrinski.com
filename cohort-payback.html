You know what a customer costs. How long until they pay you back?

Cohort payback is the number that quietly runs a subscription or repeat-purchase business: it folds acquisition cost, retention, and margin into one figure, and it decides how fast each dollar can be recycled into the next cohort. This models the curve, and shows what one point of retention is actually worth.

Retention shape

The model treats the cohort as an activation cliff followed by a steady ongoing rate, which is the shape real retention curves take and the one a single blended churn rate cannot represent. Contribution is counted per surviving customer per cycle and is not discounted, so treat long paybacks as optimistic. It is a directional model: the real version gets built from your own cohort table, by acquisition source, because retention that degrades cohort over cohort is usually a media problem wearing a retention costume.
