Getting cited is not the same as getting named. AI assistants name the brands that are a clean, resolvable entity and are corroborated across the sources the model trusts. Being the best answer on your own site is necessary and not sufficient. The lever that moves naming is off-site: a consistent entity, third-party corroboration, and original research the model has seen.

## Cited versus named: why does the distinction decide everything?

[Why your brand is not in the AI answer](/blog/why-your-brand-is-not-in-the-ai-answer/) explains the exclusion: the engine has no credible, machine-readable source to build an answer from. This is the sequel, and it turns on a distinction most brands miss. There are two different outcomes in an AI answer, and they have two different bars.

A citation is a quote. The engine finds a fact on your page, lifts it, and may link you as one source among several. Useful, but it does not make you the recommendation. A naming is an endorsement. The engine tells the user that you are the answer, the brand to consider, the expert to hire. Clearing the first bar is a content and crawlability problem. Clearing the second is an authority and entity problem, and the work is mostly not on your own site. If you are getting quoted but never recommended, you have solved the wrong half.

## The entity layer: is your brand something the model can resolve?

Before an engine will name you, it has to know what you are with confidence. That means resolving you as a single entity: one name, one bio, one positioning, and matching identifiers everywhere you appear. A model will not put an unresolved or contradictory entity in front of a user, because an endorsement it cannot stand behind is a risk. This is the shift from keywords to being a [coherent entity the engine recognizes](/blog/entities-not-keywords/), and it is deliberate work, not a side effect of publishing more.

In practice it is consistent Person and Organization schema, a canonical bio you use in the same form across your site and your profiles, a single stated positioning, and sameAs references that tie your presences together so the engine can connect them. The [four P's of AI visibility](/blog/the-4-ps-of-ai-visibility/) are built to make this intentional. The test is simple: ask an assistant who you are and what you do, and see whether it describes you accurately and the same way twice. If it hedges or contradicts itself, your entity is not resolved, and no amount of on-page content will fix that.

## The corroboration layer: why is off-site the real gate?

This is the gate most brands never reach, and it is where naming is actually won. AI answers lean on a small set of sources the models trust, and those sources are not your website. They are the places the wider web agrees in: encyclopedic references, forums, video, reviews, reputable third-party coverage. An analysis of millions of AI answers found the same handful of domains cited again and again across assistants, with Wikipedia, Reddit, and YouTube near the top. If those places do not corroborate you, your own pages are a brand talking about itself, and the engine treats that accordingly. This is the [source-of-truth gap](/blog/the-source-of-truth-gap/) applied to recommendation: the answer about who is best gets built from sources you do not own.

For a brand or an expert trying to get named, the corroboration that moves the needle is specific. Third-party best-of and roundup lists, because the "who are the best" shortlists are often synthesized straight from them. Original research or data the model has seen cited elsewhere, because it makes you a source other sources point to. A consistent presence on the platforms the engines read, including LinkedIn, which AI answer surfaces are confirmed to cite. None of that lives in your content management system, which is exactly why it is the real work, and why a brand can be technically flawless on-site and still never get named. The companion move per engine is covered in [one citation play per engine](/blog/one-citation-play-per-engine/).

## The answer layer: are you the cleanest extractable source?

The entity and the corroboration get you considered. The answer layer wins the specific question. When the engine builds a response, it lifts the clearest, most direct answer it can find and attributes it to whoever stated it cleanest, which is why answer-first pages beat pages that make the reader work. You want the extractable, quotable line on each question you intend to own to be yours, backed by the credential and the structured data that let the engine trust it. This is necessary, it just is not the lever people think it is. It decides which of the resolved, corroborated brands gets the sentence, not whether an unresolved, uncorroborated brand gets named at all. The whole picture, on-site and off, is one system, which is the argument in [SXO versus SEO, one machine](/blog/sxo-vs-seo-one-machine/).

## A 30-day order of operations

Do these in order, because each one is wasted without the one before it. First, resolve the entity: fix the name, bio, positioning, schema, and sameAs so you are one consistent thing the model can model, and confirm it by asking the assistants who you are. Second, audit naming versus citation: ask the buyer questions across engines and record where you are quoted, where you are named, and which competitors are named in your place. Third, close the corroboration gap, which is the slow, high-value work: pursue the roundup and best-of lists, publish original research worth citing, and build the consistent off-site presence, because that is what turns a resolved entity into a named one. Last, sharpen the answer layer on the handful of questions you most want to own. Measure it as presence and naming share per engine over time, not as traffic, the same way the [why your brand](/blog/why-your-brand-is-not-in-the-ai-answer/) post frames the scoreboard. So the question is not whether your content is good enough to be quoted. It is whether the web outside your site agrees on you clearly enough to be named, and have you done any of the work that lives there?
